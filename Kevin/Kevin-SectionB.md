## Problem Statement
To use a pair of scissors, it is required for you to have a thumb, to hold the scissors and separate the blades; and digit one (index finger) for support and added strength. Because of this, people who are missing their thumb and index finger on their dominant hand have difficulty holding and operating a pair of scissors, causing discomfort, and difficulty having a good hold on the scissors. No existing pair of scissors accommodate for these missing fingers: most, if not all, pairs of scissors require the thumb. There is a need to design a pair of scissors, or redesign an existing pair, to make it so people who are missing those digits can: hold the scissors easily; reliably and safely articulate the blades with little to no difficulty; and to be able to cut a variety of materials with the scissors. The cost of the scissors should be kept low, the overall size of the scissors should be close to the same as a normal pair, the scissor grips should be ergonomic and comfortable to hold, and the scissors should stay durable and sharp over a long period of time.
