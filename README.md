# Adaptive Product Design Project
Accessible product design project for Engineering Capstone

# Assignment

## The Challenge
Everyday products often rely on a user's ability to grip, pinch, and apply force with their thumb and index finger. How could an existing product be redesigned for someone who is missing these digits on one hand?

Working in pairs, design and develop an adaptation to one existing product that helps your assigned persona complete a specific task. Your group will choose a product, investigate the user's needs, generate three feasible concepts, and develop your selected design into a testable prototype.

---

## The user
Your starting persona is missing the thumb and index finger on one hand and retains the middle, ring, and little fingers.
Develop this persona by identifying:
- Which hand is affected, their goals, and the context in which they use the product.
- Relevant abilities, preferences, and difficulties.
- Information you know, assumptions you are making, and the questions requiring investigation.  

Do not assume that people with the same missing digits have the same strength, movement, sensation, or needs.
Clearly label a fictional persona and any unverified details.

## Design Requirements

Your solution must:

* Adapt an existing product while preserving its essential function.
* Address the needs of your specific persona and task.
* Consider hand contact, control, attachment, and deliberate release.
* Use materials and manufacturing processes available within the class.
* Develop through testing and at least two documented revisions.
* Allow comparison with the original product using the same test conditions.

You may explore reshaped handles, palm supports, removable attachments, alternative contact surfaces, or mechanical assistance. The final prototype must demonstrate the features needed to test your main design claims.

## Working as a Pair

Both partners must contribute to research, idea generation, modeling, testing, and communication. Share practical tasks and rotate responsibilities during development.

Keep a brief contribution record showing what each partner completed and the decisions you made together.

---

## Prototype Testing

Plan your tests when writing the specification. Choose measures suited to your task, such as:

* **Task performance:** completion time, accuracy, successful repetitions, spills, or dropped objects.
* **Control and stability:** slipping, unwanted rotation, or loss of alignment.
* **Attachment and release:** time taken, assistance required, and reliability.
* **Product compatibility:** access to controls and preservation of the original function.

Test the original product and prototype under consistent conditions. Record the setup, results, and limitations. For each revision, explain:

$$\text{What we tested} \longrightarrow \text{What we found} \longrightarrow \text{What we changed} \longrightarrow \text{Why}$$

Classmates can test mechanisms, product fit, and durability. Restricting a classmate's fingers does not establish how the intended user would experience the design. If an intended user is unavailable, clearly identify comfort and usability claims that remain unverified.

Handlebar testing must remain stationary. Use blunt-tip scissors, water in spray bottles, and a model for toothbrush demonstrations. All test setups require teacher approval.

## Final Submission

Submit:

1. A portfolio covering sections A–E.
2. Your original product, development models, and testable prototype.
3. Test results and evidence of at least two revisions.
4. Dimensioned orthographic and isometric drawings, with materials and assembly details.
5. A short demonstration explaining how the redesign compares with the original product.

> **Success means showing, through evidence, how your design addresses the user's task—and explaining what still needs development or validation.**
