# Appendix A: Persona Grip Strength Estimation

To estimate the reduced grip strength for a target persona missing an index finger and thumb, we conducted a pilot measurement on dominant-hand grip strength with and without the thumb and index finger engaged.

## Table A1: Testing Dominant Hand Grip Strength Data (n = 5)\*

*All values reported to three significant figures.*

| Participant | Strength w/o Index & Thumb (kg) | Full Grip Strength (kg) | Retained Strength (%) |
|-------------|---------------------------------|-------------------------|--------------------------|
| Noah        | 26.8 KG                         | 43.3 KG                 | 61.9% |
| Markus      | 38.9 KG                         | 66.2 KG                 | 58.8% |
| Kevin       | 24.9 KG                         | 40.2 KG                 | 61.9% |
| Corwin      | 31.7 KG                         | 43.6 KG                 | 72.7% |
| Eli         | 35.9 KG                         | 50.2 KG                 | 71.5% |
| AVERAGES    | 31.6 KG                         | 48.7 KG                 | 65.4% |

\**Methodology & Limitations:* Tests were conducted by male student participants (n = 5, age 17) using all but their thumb and index finger on their dominant hand. These tests were a rough approximation, as participants were able-bodied, younger than our persona (age 24), and represent a small sample size.

## Calculations for Persona Grip Estimate

To estimate Mr. Jarenson's grip strength:

1. **Baseline:** Published population data by Massy-Westropp et al. [1] places the average grip strength for males aged 20–29 at **47 kg**.
2. **Adjustment:** Applying our average retained strength percentage from our testing (**65.4%**) to the population baseline gives:  
  
**Estimated Grip Strength** = 47 kg × 0.654 = **30.7 kg**
